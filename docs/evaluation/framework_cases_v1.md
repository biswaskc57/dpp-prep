# Framework applicability cases — v1 (frozen before classification)

Version: v1, 2026-10-18. Author: Biswas KC. Purpose: evaluation of the readiness and response framework (thesis Chapter 3) beyond the W-100 demonstrator (thesis section 6.2).

## Note for the assessor

The seven cases below are fictional but technically plausible. They are not taken from any real company or from the thesis interview. Each case describes one information element that a manufacturer is asked to prepare for use in a digital product passport. Requirements marked as customer-specified are not claims about legal obligations.

Please assess each case with the framework in Chapter 3 (Figure 5 and the attached definitions of the eight readiness conditions, the four statuses and the five response families). Record each applicable condition with one of the four defined statuses: met, not met, not required, not yet assessed. If you cannot decide about the overall case or about a suitable response, say so in the last field of the sheet and explain why. Uncertainty is a valid result.

## Cases

**C01 — Aluminium extrusion profile, carbon footprint.**
A manufacturer of aluminium window profiles is asked for the carbon footprint of one profile type, cradle-to-gate, per kg, with the calculation method stated. In 2024 an external consultant carried out an LCA in a commercial LCA tool; the company holds only a PDF summary giving 8.9 kg CO₂e per kg for the "standard profile family". The PDF names the LCA software but does not identify the calculation method or provide the underlying inventory. It does not say whether the anodising step or the share of recycled billet were included. The requested profile belongs to that family but uses a different alloy. The consultant holds the LCA project file.

**C02 — Office chair, recycled content of the steel frame.**
A furniture manufacturer must report the recycled content of each main component, as a percentage of mass, with a supporting supplier statement. The steel supplier's annual sustainability letter states: "Our steel products contain on average 35 % recycled content." The frame tubes are bought through a distributor, so the purchase records show the distributor's article number, not the steel supplier's. A purchasing employee considers the letter sufficient.

**C03 — Control board, substances of concern.**
An electronics manufacturer must state, for each control board, whether any substance from a customer-specified list is present above a stated threshold. Declarations for 11 of the 12 component types are stored in the PLM system. For one capacitor, the supplier was asked by e-mail six weeks ago and has not replied. The supplier's previous capacitor model had a declaration showing no listed substance above the threshold. An engineer suggests that this declaration "probably still applies".

**C04 — Hydraulic pump unit, spare-part identification.**
A machinery manufacturer must list each replaceable part with one identifier and a link to the supplier's part. The same seal kit is "SK-220" in the ERP system, "100-4471-B" in the PLM system and "NBR-220/40" in the supplier's catalogue. The links between these numbers are kept in a spreadsheet of about 2,000 rows maintained by one employee; when it was last fully updated is unknown. Some mappings have been checked during purchasing, but no complete verification record is available.

**C05 — Workwear jacket, fibre composition.**
A textile manufacturer must state the fibre composition of a jacket model. The fabric supplier declared 65 % polyester and 35 % cotton in March 2025, and in February 2026 sent a revised declaration of 63 % polyester, 35 % cotton and 2 % elastane, "applying to new production lots". The warehouse holds jackets made from both fabric lots; the lot is recorded on the production order but not linked to the sales item number. The current product page shows the 2025 composition. The customer has not specified whether one composition must cover the model or whether different production lots may be identified separately.

**C06 — Corrugated box, dimensions and basis weight.**
A packaging manufacturer must provide, for each box type, the outer dimensions in mm and the basis weight in g/m² of the finished corrugated board used. Both values are numeric fields with units in the ERP item master, maintained by product engineering and compared weekly with production measurements. The customer's requested format uses the same fields and units as the ERP export.

**C07 — Battery module housing, production energy per unit.**
A manufacturer of aluminium battery housings is asked for the electricity used in production per housing, as input to a customer's LCA. The plant's energy monitoring system gives 14.2 kWh per unit, averaged over the first quarter of the year. The LCA input sheet prepared last year by the company's LCA analyst gives 11.8 kWh per unit, based on an engineering estimate. No document states which figure is the official value; the energy manager and the LCA analyst each consider their own figure correct.

## Protocol (fixed before any classification)

1. This file is frozen before any case is classified. Later changes are recorded as a new version with the reason.
2. Cases for the second assessor (Mayte), chosen before classification: **C04 and C07**.
3. The author classifies all seven cases. The second assessor classifies C04 and C07 without seeing the author's classifications. Her original sheets are kept unchanged.
4. The two assessments are compared condition by condition. Disagreements are reported as they are, not resolved in favour of the author. No agreement coefficient is calculated (two cases are too few).
5. The second assessor contributed to the wider thesis as a supervisor, so her assessment is expert feedback, not fully independent validation. If she cannot take part, the assessment is reported as author-led, without independent checking.
6. No answers are defined as correct in advance. The evaluation asks whether the framework lets an assessor:
   - identify the relevant information requirements and readiness gaps;
   - distinguish confirmed gaps from uncertainty;
   - identify plausible response families without assuming that AI is necessary;
   - explain which controls are required;
   - trace the reasoning to the case facts.

   A case where the framework shows uncertainty or several plausible responses is not a failure. The framework fails a case when it does not help the assessor state the uncertainty, distinguish the relevant conditions, or explain what further evidence or decision is needed.
7. Limits of the claim: seven fictional cases can show that the framework is applicable and interpretable in several manufacturing contexts. They do not show industrial effectiveness, general validity or that the framework selects an optimal response. The author designed both the cases and the framework; freezing and the second assessment reduce, but do not remove, this bias.
